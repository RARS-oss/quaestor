01 The gap Alpaca is standing on

The rails exist. The trust layer doesn't.

Agents already place orders through Alpaca's Trading API. What none of them can produce is a trustworthy record of what they actually did — and that missing record is the thing every downstream market needs.

  • ›An agent's “+40% this month” is unverifiable. The run could be cherry-picked, backdated, executed with lookahead, or simply fabricated. The operator's word is the only link between the claim and the exchange.
  • ›That single missing primitive blocks the next wave: an agent marketplace, copy-trading of AI agents, and the ability to follow, fund, or audit an autonomous strategy on evidence rather than reputation.
  • ›It also blocks the unglamorous but mandatory parts — compliance-grade execution logs, dispute resolution, and answering “what exactly did this agent do, and when” with something a regulator accepts.
Why Alpaca

Alpaca is the broker of record for exactly the API-first, agent-first cohort where this problem lands first. Whoever defines “provable agent track record” owns the rails the copy-trading and marketplace wave runs on. That standard is far more natural to set from the brokerage layer than to bolt on afterward.

02 What we built

The primitive, concretely

Not a slide. A running system: orders placed under seal over a mediated tunnel, signed into a tamper-evident ledger, provable end to end with no credentials and no server.

Sealed live trading

Orders are placed from inside a no-root hermetic Linux cell (user/pid/mount/net namespaces, pivot_root, seccomp) over a mediated egress tunnel. TLS terminates in-cell — keys never reach the broker in plaintext — and every HTTPS CONNECT is hash-chained into the receipt. Seal stays HELD.

Signed, tamper-evident ledger

Each cycle is Ed25519-signed and hash-chained into a weekly ledger (receipts/sealed-ledger.jsonl). Edit one field and the signature rejects it; drop one cycle and the chain breaks. The policy.yaml digest is bound in — silently loosening the rules is visible.

Zero-knowledge risk caps

Each order can carry a Bulletproofs range proof that its worst-case loss is under a hard cap — < 2^16 USD — verifiable without revealing position size. The Pedersen commitment prefix is embedded in the Alpaca client_order_id, binding the on-exchange order to its own risk proof.

External anchor

Every cycle's ledger head is appended to a self-chaining anchor log (runs/anchor.jsonl) and can be witnessed to an append-only external log — opaque hashes only. This closes the tail-truncation hole: even deleting the newest losing day is caught.

Browser-only WASM verifier

The exact Rust checker that validates the signature, body digest and event chain, compiled to WebAssembly and shipped in a single self-contained HTML file. Paste a receipt, hit Verify — no server, no install. Change one character and watch it reject.

attested-alpaca — a drop-in layer

The whole primitive as a wrapper any agent adopts in a few lines. Wrap your paper credentials; submit_sealed() returns an order plus a signed, verifiable receipt. A ~30-line example agent ships in examples/minimal_agent.py.

order = aa.submit_sealed({                          # placed inside a sealed cell

03 What it unlocks for Alpaca

Alpaca Verified Agents

The same primitive, framed as product. A standard, a verifier, and a track-record surface — the trust rails an agent economy on Alpaca would be built on.

A standard + verifierthe receipt format

A published receipt schema and an offline verifier (native + WASM) that any third party runs with nothing but a public key. This is the interoperable unit that makes agent performance portable across the ecosystem instead of locked in one operator's dashboard.

Verified track-record pagesprovable performance

A per-agent surface of signed, anchored, in-browser-verifiable P&L. “Provable agent performance” is the number a follower can act on — the basis for a marketplace listing or a copy-trading decision that rests on evidence, not the operator's word.

Marketplace & copy-trading trustthe next wave

Follow, fund, copy, or audit an autonomous agent on the strength of a signature. Verifiable execution is the missing precondition that lets Alpaca host that layer credibly instead of ceding it to a third party.

Compliance-grade logsaudit & dispute

A signed, hash-chained, externally anchored record of every order and the exact request/response transcript behind it — with the governing policy digest bound in. The artifact you want when the question is “what did this agent do, and under what rules.”

To be exact about what this is: a reference implementation and a thesis, not a finished marketplace. What's demonstrated end-to-end is the hard part — sealed placement, signed receipts, offline verification. The product surfaces above are the natural build-out, sketched honestly, not claimed as shipped.

05 Provenance & honest scope

What's real, and what isn't yet

quaestor's agent, strategy and integration code was written during the hackathon (Aug 28 – Sep 4, 2026). It deliberately builds on two of our own pre-existing, MIT-licensed research projects, both published before the event.

  • ›bulla — the hermetic no-root sandbox that emits signed, offline-verifiable execution receipts (and the Bulletproofs risk proofs). github.com/RARS-oss/bulla
  • ›sbx — the hermetic executor returning typed verdicts to coding agents, used in our own dev loop for this project. github.com/RARS-oss/sbx

That's the point, stated plainly: verifiable-governance infrastructure we'd already researched for AI agents, pointed at the place it matters most — an autonomous agent that trades real order flow on Alpaca's rails.

Real, running today

  • Sealed order placement over a mediated tunnel; seal HELD, verified.
  • Ed25519-signed receipts, hash-chained ledger, external anchor.
  • Zero-knowledge risk-cap proofs bound to the client_order_id.
  • Native + browser-only WASM verifiers; make verify offline.
  • attested-alpaca drop-in wrapper with a working example agent.

Future work — not claimed

  • Paper-trading only. No live capital; fail-closed paper gate.
  • A hosted marketplace, listings, and copy-trading UX.
  • A hardened, standardized receipt spec with versioning.
  • Multi-operator key management and revocation.
  • Regulatory sign-off on the log as a compliance artifact.
