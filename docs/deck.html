01 · The gap

The rails exist. The trust layer doesn't.

Agents already trade on Alpaca's API. None of them can produce a record of what they actually did.

  • A screenshot saying "+40% this month" could be cherry-picked, backdated, or invented. The operator's word is the only thing tying the claim to the exchange.
  • That missing piece blocks everything downstream: an agent marketplace, copy-trading, compliance-grade execution logs.
  • You cannot fund, follow, or audit a strategy on reputation alone.
02 · The agent

Autonomous options, every five minutes

Multi-leg, inside a sealed cell
  • Regime gates the playbook. Storm stands down. Range sells premium. Trend buys convexity.
  • Every intent is judged before it reaches the exchange — per-trade loss caps, concentration, spread quality, 0DTE cutoffs, a daily halt that flattens the book.
  • Premium is never held overnight. The stop is the risk control on a short spread, and a gap steps over a stop.
03 · The primitive

The order is not placed by the agent

It is placed from inside a hermetic, no-root cell — no network, no filesystem, no ambient credentials.

TLS terminates inside the seal

The cell reaches Alpaca through a mediated tunnel. The broker never sees the keys, and every call is hash-chained into the record.

The receipt is the exchange transcript

Ed25519-signed, covering the exact conversation: which contract, which price, which response.

Receipts chain into a ledger

Tamper-evident, and its head is witnessed to an external anchor.

Both edits are caught

Change one number and the signature rejects it. Drop one losing day and the chain breaks.

  • Offline, no credentials. The same check compiled to WebAssembly runs entirely in the judge's browser.
  • Paste a receipt — verified. Change a single character — rejected. No server, no trust, no API call.
  • Deterministic replay. Each decision seals the inputs it was judged on and the policy it was judged under, so every approve and reject re-derives from signed data.
05 · The guard

The safety claims are tested, not asserted

  • The policy's own sha256 is signed into every receipt. Loosening the rules mid-week is cryptographically visible in the audit trail.
  • The worst case is derived, not believed. Every dollar cap is measured against a number re-derived from the OCC strikes — a spread that under-reports its own risk is refused, so a sizing bug cannot be signed as fact.
  • A zero-knowledge range proof rides in the Alpaca client_order_id: worst-case loss under a hard cap, size hidden.
  • 205 tests across the gates, the strategy, the receipts and the replay path.

Four fraud vectors — a forged verdict, an edited receipt, a dropped ledger line, a faked risk proof — each demonstrated being caught, in scripts/red_team.sh.
